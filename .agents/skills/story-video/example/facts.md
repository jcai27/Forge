# Facts: The Box

Sources read for this example (Wikipedia, via its API, on the day it was written):
"Containerization", "SS Ideal X", "Malcom McLean". The example exists to show
the format; a video meant for an audience should go to the sources behind them.

| # | Claim as worded | Tag | Source | Passage it rests on | Second source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Almost everything you own came to you inside a steel box | VIEW | Rhetorical opening | | |
| 2 | Before the box, cargo was loaded by hand | SOURCE | Malcom McLean | "most cargoes were loaded and unloaded by hand by longshore workers" | Containerization: dock workers "handled break bulk cargo" |
| 3 | It was slow, heavy and expensive | SOURCE | Malcom McLean | "Containerization also greatly reduced the time to load and unload ships"; the cost figures in row 8 | |
| 4 | A trucking man named Malcom McLean; why not lift the whole trailer | SOURCE | Containerization | "American trucking entrepreneur McLean put 58 trailer vans later called containers, aboard a refitted tanker ship" | The question itself is a dramatisation, not a quotation, and is not shown in quotation marks |
| 5 | April 1956, the Ideal X left Newark for Houston | SOURCE | SS Ideal X | "on 26 April 1956, the Ideal X carried 58 containers from Port Newark, New Jersey, to Port of Houston, Texas" | Containerization, same date and ports |
| 6 | Ten years later, containers were crossing the Atlantic (1966) | SOURCE | Malcom McLean | "In April 1966, Sea-Land commenced service between New York and Rotterdam, Netherlands; Bremen, Germany; and Grangemouth, Scotland" | |

Images are captioned as what they show: a Liberty ship's hold (U.S. Navy
museum photograph), longshoremen at Hallstavik, Sweden, in 1962, container
cranes at Antwerp, a cargo ship at sea, and footage of a container ship
under way (shown without a caption, as a container ship and nothing more). None is presented
as the Ideal X or as Newark in 1956.
